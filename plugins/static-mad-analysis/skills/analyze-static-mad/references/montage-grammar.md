# Montage and compositing grammar

## Read adjacency as syntax

Describe an important edit in three layers:

1. **Denotation** — what is visibly present.
2. **Relation** — how neighboring shots connect.
3. **Function** — what the connection changes in knowledge, emotion, agency, or expectation.

Example: `eye -> medicine bottle` denotes an eye and bottle, relates through symbolic association, and functions to recast feeling as diagnosis.

## Separate the still composition from added motion

Inspect the base frame first:

- hierarchy and focal path;
- foreground, middle ground, background;
- negative space and off-screen implication;
- screen direction and relational distance;
- text legibility and reading order;
- color, value, edge, texture, and light coherence.

Then ask what camera, layer, mask, typography, light, or puppet motion contributes. Its contribution may be narrative, perceptual, rhythmic, atmospheric, decorative, or openly spectacular. Judge control and integration before deciding that non-narrative motion is empty busyness.

## Material operations

- `selection_or_crop`: isolate, reframe, or withhold part of an existing image;
- `separation_or_redraw`: split body/scene layers, reconstruct hidden regions, or restore resolution;
- `compositing_or_reconstruction`: combine elements into a coherent new spatial or graphic arrangement;
- `fabricated_composite`: synthesize elements from separate images into one apparent shot;
- `external_asset`: introduce live action, 3D, texture, archive, interface, or stock material;
- `treatment_only`: recolor, grade, texture, blur, glow, grain, or distort without changing depicted event.

Do not call every transformed image a new event. Describe only the construction that is visible or supported by available layer/source evidence.

## Common operations and questions

- **Parallax/layer animation**: is depth meaningful or only simulated?
- **Camera/crop motion**: does it reveal, search, isolate, connect, or merely drift?
- **Mask reveal**: does it enact disclosure, concealment, infection, fracture, or decoration?
- **Luma/threshold/inversion**: does polarity alter agency or valence?
- **Ink/liquid wipe**: does one state invade, erase, or transform another?
- **Screen-within-screen**: who observes, who is observed, and who controls the frame?
- **Typography**: is it narrative text, thought, lyric echo, label, diagnosis, interface, or texture?
- **Graphic match**: what persists—shape, line, direction, value, scale, or color—and what changes?
- **Repetition**: is it recall, reappraisal, obsession, accumulation, bookend, refrain, or economy?
- **Body fragmentation**: do eyes, hands, mouth, wounds, or silhouettes relocate agency?
- **Negative space**: what is absent, and does direction imply an off-screen partner?
- **Compositing/photography treatment**: do depth, blur, light, glow, aberration, grain, and overlays belong to one visual world?

## Global expression systems

Track motifs as evolving systems, not fixed dictionaries. A color, door, border, line treatment, repeated panel, or interface may change function over time.

For each system, record:

- first function and context;
- transformations and recurrences;
- relation to character state and music position;
- final function;
- counterexamples that prevent a one-word symbol assignment.

The same source image can represent an event, memory, corrected recognition, accumulated time, or final acceptance depending on its later framing.

## Processing density and “hand count”

Separate:

- visual-change density: how frequently pixels and compositions change;
- processing density: how much compositing, lighting, texture, typography, and layer work is visible;
- semantic density: how much new story or thematic information appears;
- reading demand: how much time the viewer needs to understand it.

High processing density may mark present time, pressure, control, maturity, or spectacle; low density may mark memory, intimacy, emptiness, or relief. Infer the local opposition from recurrence rather than applying a universal rule.

## Focalization

Support a focalizer with seeing/being-seen chains, knowledge restriction, reactions, memory triggers, subjective distortion, recurring eye/camera motifs, and whose uncertainty the edit answers. The largest face is not automatically the focalizer.
