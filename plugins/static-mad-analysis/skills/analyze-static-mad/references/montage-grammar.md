# Montage grammar for static MAD

## Read adjacency as syntax

Describe each important edit in three layers:

1. denotation: what is visibly present;
2. relation: how the neighboring shots connect;
3. function: what the connection changes in understanding or emotion.

Example: `eye → medicine bottle` denotes an eye and a bottle; relates through symbolic association; functions to medicalize a subjective feeling.

## Common static-MAD operations

- Layer/parallax animation: infer depth only when layers move at meaningfully different rates.
- Mask reveal: ask whether revelation, concealment, infection, fragmentation, or mere decoration is enacted.
- Luma/threshold/inversion: compare whether polarity change reverses agency or emotional valence.
- Ink/liquid wipe: test whether one image appears to invade or erase another.
- Screen-within-screen: identify observer, observed object, and who controls the frame.
- Typography: classify as readable narrative, attributed thought, lyric echo, label/diagnosis, or visual texture.
- Graphic match: track shape, direction, value, color, and scale across the cut.
- Repetition: treat as possible recall, reappraisal, obsession, bookend, or rhythmic refrain before calling it reuse.
- Body fragmentation: eyes, hands, mouth, and wounds may redistribute agency and subjectivity.
- Negative space: determine who or what is absent and whether screen direction implies an off-screen partner.

## Focalization clues

A focalizer is supported by seeing/being-seen chains, knowledge restriction, memory triggers, subjective distortion, recurring eye or camera motifs, reaction shots, and whose uncertainty the edit answers. Visibility alone is insufficient.

## Semantic density

Separate visual change rate from semantic information rate. Many flashes can repeat one proposition; one held image can introduce a decisive event. Report both when judging pacing.
