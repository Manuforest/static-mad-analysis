# Material economy and staged reveal

Use this module when limited CGs, manga panels, sprites, or absent viewpoint characters visibly shape the edit. Track how the arrangement changes access to material; do not treat source-asset count as a quality measure.

## Track asset identity and disclosure

Separate the source asset from each edited use. For an important recurrence, record internally:

`source asset -> crop or visible region -> disclosure stage -> prior use -> newly available information -> changed event or relationship -> later payoff`

Useful disclosure stages include partial, expanded, full, recontextualized, and coda reuse. These are descriptive states, not a required sequence. A full image may appear early and still gain a new function later.

Ask whether a crop guides attention, withholds a partner or cause, isolates a response, delays spatial understanding, or simply varies the surface. When the frame later expands, state what the viewer can now understand that was unavailable before.

## Read privileged-image allocation

Treat decisive expressions, revelations, deaths, embraces, group images, and route-defining CGs as potentially privileged material. Examine:

- when the strongest information first becomes available;
- whether an early use exhausts the image or establishes material for later revision;
- how music, text, duration, adjacency, and crop change its later force;
- whether the climax receives a new image, a completed image, or a changed reading of an old one;
- what the ending preserves, releases, or reopens.

Do not require a climax to reveal a full CG. Judge the allocation against the work's actual progression.

## Establish an absent subject

When a protagonist or viewpoint character lacks a sprite, test whether the edit establishes presence through:

- camera position or first-person framing;
- another character's gaze or action directed toward the camera;
- a hand, letter, photograph, desk, doorway, empty seat, or object exchanged across the frame;
- negative space with a stable implied partner;
- dialogue, address, or reaction that consistently locates the missing person.

Describe how these cues form a relation. Do not invent an off-screen subject from empty space alone.

## Follow substitute continuity

Limited material may remain continuous through gaze, direction, focal position, scale, color, typography placement, recurring objects, sound bridges, or musical phrases. For each bridge, identify what persists and what changes.

Separate surface unity from narrative continuity. Consistent grading or layout may make heterogeneous material look related while leaving an event, response, motivation, or relationship unsupported. Conversely, a visible rupture may be purposeful when the next passage makes it consequential.

## Reader-facing translation

Keep asset IDs, recurrence counts, crop labels, and disclosure states internal. In the response, describe what the viewer is allowed to see, what remains withheld, how a later appearance changes the earlier one, and why that timing matters.
