# Subject-centered video-understanding core

Use this model for static MAD, AMV, ASMV, MV, and hybrids. It describes what must be understood; type adapters describe how the evidence is produced.

## 1. Blind global pass

Inspect the work chronologically without source-plot material. Record provisional chapters, musical and emotional energy, recurring entities and motifs, unresolved identities, information-density changes, and questions the edit later answers. Use neutral labels such as `person_a` until identity is anchored.

## 2. Functional chapters

Place a chapter boundary when at least two signals change: focalizer, dramatic question, location logic, color/light, typography, motif behavior, editing density, music phrase, instrumentation, energy, dialogue function, or viewer knowledge.

Give each chapter an observable task: premise-sharing, trigger, development, conflict, accumulation, reversal, release, closure, aftertaste, or another locally supported function. Musical sections and narrative chapters may overlap without matching one-to-one.

## 3. Entities and subject roles

Track entities with multiple anchors: face, clothing, accessory, pose, voice, screen direction, associated object, interaction partner, location, and adjacent action.

Distinguish:

1. **visual subject** — highest momentary salience;
2. **shot subject** — what composition and camera treatment organize;
3. **action subject** — who or what initiates change;
4. **focalizer** — whose seeing, knowing, memory, or uncertainty structures access;
5. **dramatic subject** — whose state changes;
6. **thematic subject** — the relationship, idea, group, place, emotion, or force under examination;
7. **discourse subject** — whose proposition, judgment, or address organizes the passage when language leads.

Roles may diverge, migrate, disappear, or remain collective. Never invent a single protagonist to simplify a distributed work.

## 4. Event-response-state chains

For each meaningful event, record:

`event -> subject response -> state or relationship change -> consequence`

A response may be facial, bodily, spatial, auditory, verbal, typographic, symbolic, delayed, displaced, or intentionally absent. Mark a causal gap when the edit provides no support rather than supplying psychology from familiarity with the source.

Classify the larger topology as single-thread transformation, dual-thread relation, multi-thread convergence, episodic accumulation, fragmented association, or a chapter-specific hybrid.

## 5. Time-space and relation reconstruction

Classify meaningful shot relations before forming chronology. Test physical continuity against temporal, subjective, graphic, symbolic, causal, reactive, and sound-based alternatives. Read [temporal-relations.md](temporal-relations.md) when the relation remains ambiguous or the work uses dense nonlinear construction.

Maintain six clocks independently:

- playback time;
- shot time;
- story time;
- subjective time;
- music time;
- viewer-disclosure time.

Inspect the preceding shot, boundary, following shot, audio bridge, and later recurrence for important transitions.

## 6. Audiovisual organization

Treat sound as an organizing stream, not accompaniment. Separate dialogue/narration, lyrics, music, effects, ambience, silence, and transitions. Track who hears what, what the viewer hears, and which sound bridges otherwise discontinuous images.

Distinguish beat, phrase, energy, lyric-semantic, structural, dialogue-rhetorical, and contrapuntal alignment. A synchronized cut is not automatically a narrative beat. Examine motion amplitude, easing, holds, reading time, information density, accumulation, release, and dynamic/static contrast.

## 7. Editorial thesis and interpretation testing

Generate at least two interpretations of what the edit organizes as important. Each needs:

- a concrete claim;
- timestamped support across more than one interval;
- counterevidence;
- a plausible alternative;
- confidence;
- an interval whose closer inspection could change the conclusion.

Prefer the interpretation that explains subject-role changes, event chains, recurrences, audiovisual structure, and ending with the fewest unsupported assumptions. Preserve ambiguity when different readings explain different layers.

## 8. Three-layer synthesis

After completing the blind core, add original-work context and creator expression in that order. Use [original-work-context.md](original-work-context.md) to map relevant source facts to editorial transformation. Keep the three outputs distinct: video body, original-work context, and creator expression.

## 9. Evidence discipline

Preserve observation, video-grounded inference, original-work context, other external context, and documented creator intent as separate provenance classes. Use [evidence-schema.md](evidence-schema.md) when auditable records are needed. Type adapters may change how evidence is produced, never the required support.
