# Text-led narrative in static MAD

Use this module when readable text carries substantial event, causality, psychology, viewpoint, relationship, temporal, or emotional work. Do not treat low text volume as restraint by default or high text volume as excess by default.

## Identify the text situation

For consequential text, distinguish source dialogue, character monologue, editor-authored narration, third-person narration, letter or document, lyric, quotation, graphic text, and interface or metadata. Establish where possible:

`voice or source -> addressee -> visible subject -> who can hear or know it -> discourse task`

Keep voice uncertain when the edit does not identify it. The visible character does not automatically speak, hear, endorse, or receive the text.

## Track four progressions

Use four internal progressions together:

1. **Information**: what event, cause, time, relationship, or fact becomes available?
2. **Reading demand**: can the relevant viewer read and absorb it under the duration, motion, contrast, size, hierarchy, and competing detail?
3. **Emotion**: does the passage accumulate, hesitate, repeat, redirect, release, or settle?
4. **Image-text labor**: do text and image supplement, deepen, counterpoint, relay, confirm, pause, or compete?

Do not publish counts or curves in ordinary analysis. Convert them into the actual reading experience.

## Name the text's work

Text may narrate an event, supply causality, expose psychology, restrict focalization, bridge time, define a relationship, delay a revelation, pace emotion, establish a refrain, or operate graphically. A line may perform several tasks; identify the task that changes the passage.

Repetition is not automatically redundant. Test whether changed speaker, image, addressee, timing, or prior knowledge turns the repeated line into confirmation, self-correction, irony, insistence, pause, or payoff.

## Judge dense text fairly

High-density text can remain clear and moving when:

- voice and addressee remain legible;
- successive lines change information, psychology, or relationship;
- line breaks and display time fit the way the thought unfolds;
- the image gives priority to reading when required;
- decisive sentences receive enough visual and musical space;
- recurring lines change under later context;
- the climax answers or transforms a question accumulated through the text.

Diagnose a problem through its consequence: missed causality, unstable voice, unreadable timing, emotional stasis, image-text competition, or illustration that removes useful tension. Pure illustration can still be appropriate for a letter, reading scene, visual-novel register, or deliberate confirmation.

## Reader-facing translation

Write about what a line reveals, whose state it changes, how the image answers it, and whether the music lets it land. Avoid “text density,” “information throughput,” character counts, reading-speed estimates, or general prescriptions to reduce copy unless the user requests a method audit or revision advice.
